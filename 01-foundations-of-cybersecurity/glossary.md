## Terms and definitions from Course 1

<span style="color: #4285F4">A</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Adversarial artificial intelligence:** A technique that manipulates artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) technology to conduct attacks more efficiently</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Antivirus software:** A software program used to prevent, detect, and eliminate malware and viruses</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Asset:** An item perceived as having value to an organization</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Availability:** ==The idea that data is accessible== to those who are authorized to access it</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">B</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Business Email Compromise (BEC):** ==A type of phishing attack where a threat actor impersonates a known source to obtain financial advantage==</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">C</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Cloud security:** The process of ensuring that assets stored in the cloud are properly configured and access to those assets is limited to authorized users</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Compliance:** The process of adhering to internal standards and external regulations</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Computer virus:** Malicious code written to interfere with computer operations and cause damage to data and software</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Confidentiality:** Only authorized users can access specific assets or data</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Confidentiality, integrity, availability (CIA) triad:** A model that helps inform how organizations consider risk when setting up systems and security policies</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Cryptographic attack:** An attack that affects secure forms of communication between a sender and intended recipient</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Cybersecurity (or security):** The practice of ensuring confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information by protecting networks, devices, people, and data from unauthorized access or criminal exploitation</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">D</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Database:** An organized collection of information or data</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Data point:** A specific piece of information</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">H</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Hacker:** ==Any person who uses computers to gain access to computer systems, networks, or data==</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Hacktivist:** A person who uses hacking to achieve a political goal</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA):** A U.S. federal law established to protect patients’ health information</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">I</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Integrity:** The idea that the data is correct, authentic, and reliable</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Internal threat:** A current or former employee, external vendor, or trusted partner who poses a security risk</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Intrusion detection system (IDS):** An application that monitors system activity and alerts on possible intrusions</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">L</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Linux:** An open-source operating system</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Log:** A record of events that occur within an organization’s systems</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">M</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Malware:** Software designed to harm devices or networks</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">N</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Cyber Security Framework (CSF):** A voluntary framework that consists of standards, guidelines, and best practices to manage cybersecurity risk</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Network protocol analyzer (packet sniffer):** ==A tool designed to capture and analyze data traffic within a network==</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Network security:** The practice of keeping an organization's network infrastructure secure from unauthorized access</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">O</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Open Web Application Security Project (OWASP):** A non-profit organization focused on improving software security</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Order of volatility:** ==A sequence outlining the order of data that must be preserved from first to last==</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">P</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Password attack:** An attempt to access password secured devices, systems, networks, or data</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Personally identifiable information (PII):** Any information used to infer an individual’s identity</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Phishing:** The use of digital communications to trick people into revealing sensitive data or deploying malicious software</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Physical attack:** A security incident that affects not only digital but also physical environments where the incident is deployed</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Physical social engineering:** An attack in which a threat actor impersonates an employee, customer, or vendor to obtain unauthorized access to a physical location</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Privacy protection:** The act of safeguarding personal information from unauthorized use</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Programming:** ==A process that can be used to create a specific set of instructions for a computer to execute tasks==</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Protected health information (PHI):** Information that relates to the past, present, or future physical or mental health or condition of an individual</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Protecting and preserving evidence:** ==The process of properly working with fragile and volatile digital evidence==</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">S</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Security architecture:** A type of security design composed of multiple components, such as tools and processes, that are used to protect an organization from risks and external threats</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Security controls:** Safeguards designed to reduce specific security risks</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Security ethics:** Guidelines for making appropriate decisions as a security professional</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Security frameworks:** Guidelines used for building plans to help mitigate risk and threats to data and privacy</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Security governance:** Practices that help support, define, and direct security efforts of an organization</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**==Security information and event management (SIEM)==**==:== An application that collects and analyzes log data to monitor critical activities in an organization</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Security posture:** An organization’s ability to manage its defense of critical assets and data and react to change</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Sensitive personally identifiable information (SPII):** A specific type of PII that falls under stricter handling guidelines</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Social engineering:** A manipulation technique that exploits human error to gain private information, access, or valuables</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Social media phishing:** A type of attack where a threat actor collects detailed information about their target on social media sites before initiating the attack</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Spear phishing:** ==A malicious email attack targeting a specific user or group of users, appearing to originate from a trusted source==</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**SQL (Structured Query Language):** A programming language used to create, interact with, and request information from a database</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Supply-chain attack:** ==An attack that targets systems, applications,== hardware, ==and/or software to locate a vulnerability where malware can be deployed==</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">T</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Technical skills:** Skills that require knowledge of specific tools, procedures, and policies</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Threat:** Any circumstance or event that can negatively impact assets</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Threat actor:** Any person or group who presents a security risk</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Transferable skills:** Skills from other areas that can apply to different careers</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">U</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**USB baiting:** ==An attack in which a threat actor strategically leaves a malware USB stick for an employee to find== and install to unknowingly infect a network</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">V</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**Virus:** refer to ==“computer virus”==</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**==Vishing:==** ==The exploitation of electronic voice communication to obtain sensitive information or to impersonate a known source==</span>

<span style="color: #4285F4">W</span>

<span style="color: #434343">**==Watering hole attack==**==:== A type of attack when a threat actor compromises a website frequently visited by a specific group of users</span>
